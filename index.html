VaakBoard

Typing Sanskrit should feel like typing, not transliterating.

An iOS keyboard with its own input engine. Latin letters in, correct Devanāgarī out, with an offline Sanskrit lexicon behind it. This isn’t a restyled layout.

Try these (IAST works too)

Case matters in the Latin scheme: M is anusvāra, H visarga, R is ṛ, T D N are retroflex, aa ii uu are long vowels. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

The problem

Typing Sanskrit on a phone isn’t a layout problem.

It’s phonetics, transliteration, the rules of Devanāgarī and fast lookup, all inside the limits of a phone keyboard.

  • Phonetics

    Type the sounds. The engine finds the letters.

  • Transliteration

    Latin and IAST in, Devanāgarī out, and back again.

  • Script rules

    A consonant carries a virama until a vowel replaces it with the right matra.

  • Local lookup

    A custom trie and an offline lexicon, so typing never waits on a network.

Under the hoodHow the engine gets from letters to a word

These five stages are the demo’s actual code, so the values update as you type above.

  1. 1Input

    saMskRtam

    Raw characters, as typed.

  2. 2Normalise

    Latin scheme

    Unicode forms made equal, IAST detected.

  3. 3Tokenise

    s · a · M · s · k · R · t · a · m

    Longest match first: three letters, then two, then one.

  4. 4Compose

    स् अ ं स् क् ृ त् अ म्

    Consonants take a virama; a vowel replaces it with a matra.

  5. 5Output

    संस्कृतम्

    Devanāgarī, plus IAST on request.
